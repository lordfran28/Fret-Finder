## 23 November 2026 (night)

- **I'm in the Mood for Love, bars 13 and 37.** D major 9's picking struck only the A and G strings, so its F♯, the 3rd, was never heard. *Why:* the hits were read from the record's piano, which plays D, A and C♯ there, and the root-and-3rd rule gave way to Rod's G passing over F♯ for a fifth of a second. *Rule:* a passing sung note under half a second doesn't excuse a pattern without its 3rd; strike it where the voice has moved on. Now the second hit takes the D string, as bar 5 does. *Check:* `nokey/rv.py SLUG` ("the pattern never sounds the 3rd").
- **I'm in the Mood for Love, bar 52.** A13's F♯ was struck twice under the G Rod holds at the end of the bar, a half step above it; the record's band has no F♯ there (A, C♯, B, G). Bar 28, the same place in the first bridge, already left it out. *Rule:* a grip tone a half step from a held sung note stays only where the record's band plays it too; the same place in a repeated section gets the same answer. *Check:* `nokey/vc.py`, then `nokey/band.py` on the separated instrumental.

- **Our Love Is Here to Stay (not changed: needs his yes).** Five chords have two grips each (G minor 9, C13, F major 9, C9, C7♭9), so the same place in two A sections is played two ways, and C13 jumps to the 8th fret between chords at the 3rd and 1st (bars 19 and 33). *Why:* the 19 November rebuild chose grips bar by bar from the record's piano, before one grip per chord was run on this song. *Rule:* one grip per chord for the whole song (CLAUDE.md). ## 17 November 2026 (evening, continued)

- **Après un rêve, Soave sia il vento, the 1898 lullaby: one-beat chords.** Each song's figure gives a beat three or four strokes and saves one voice for the second beat, so a chord that lasts one beat never sounded it: Fauré's inner voice (D half-diminished over G without D, G7 over B without G, C7♭9 without E, in 27 bars), Mozart's bass-or-inner (D over A without D, E minor without E, B7 over A without B), the lullaby's G7 over C without B. ## 18 November 2026 (evening): a falling bass, done twice

- **A suspension on every step of a walk-down is too much.** In It Was Almost Like a Song, bar 13, each top note hung over the next bass note and sighed a half step down, four times in a row. The owner: it "didn't make it more beautiful, but less". A device repeated on every beat becomes a mechanism, and one shape still slid down the neck under it. *Rule:* milk a falling bass with motion the ear can follow as a line, not one trick per step: break each chord across the strings with its colour (9ths, 11ths, 13ths that the record's chords allow), let a common open string ring through several chords, and make the top climb against the bass (contrary motion). Save a suspension for the one place where the fall lands. Bar 13 is now G♯m9, G13, F♯m11, F9 into E major 9, broken upward, the open B ringing, the top climbing B, E, A, then G and F♯.
- **A splice into the singer's track needs the guitar's level in that track.** The singer's track is the guitar plus the voice, normalised as a whole, so the guitar inside it is not at the level of the guitar-only track (0.55 of it in Long Ago and Far Away, 0.97 in I'll Be Seeing You). *Rule:* to set new bars into an old singer's track, measure the guitar's scale where the singer is silent (the intro) and add the change at that scale; where the new render's level differs from the old track's (Long Ago and Far Away's old track was made louder), match the new bars to the old over notes both versions share, and splice only from where the notes change.

## 19 November 2026 (night): the lute figure on the Rod Stewart pages

- **I Only Have Eyes for You and That Old Feeling: the figure skipped the chord's 3rd.** The figure plucks four of a grip's five or six notes, and its tenor was simply the lowest string above the bass, whatever note that held; so A major 9 was heard without its C♯ and E minor 9 without its G, in many bars, while the diagrams showed them. *Why:* the figure was built from string positions, not from what the notes are. *Rule:* the note under the line is the chord's 3rd, or else its 7th, or over another bass its root; a chord of one beat is struck whole. *Check:* `review.py SLUG` ("the pattern never sounds"); the kit's `rk.brise` now chooses the tenor this way for every new page.
