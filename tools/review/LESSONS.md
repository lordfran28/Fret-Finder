# Lessons

Each mistake, why it happened, and the rule (and check) that prevents it. Newest first.

## 17 November 2026 (evening, continued)

- **Key Finder, Cloudy, bars 18, 34, 56 and 64.** He holds C♯ over D minor (D minor with its major 7th on the guitar page) and, after the key change, D over E♭ minor. The beginner chart made both minor 7th chords, so their C and D♭ sat a half step from his note. *Why:* the beginner chart reads D minor (major 7) as plain D minor, then keeps one kind of D minor for the whole song, and the song's other D minors are minor 7ths. *Rule:* folding a song's chords into one kind per root must not give a chord a 7th that fights the melody; where it would, keep the plain chord (`OVERRIDE_BARS` in `tools/piano/beginner.py`). *Check:* `review.py SLUG --kf` (rubs).
- **The review on older pages.** If I Ever Lose This Heaven keeps its bars inside sections and names its tracks in its data, so the review couldn't read it, and Cloudy's “With the record's voice” was reported as a singer's track with the wrong guitar take. *Rule:* the review reads both older forms, and a track named as the record's voice is the record's own singer, not a mix. *Check:* `review.py if-i-ever-lose-this-heaven --voice --sound`.

- **The Nearness of You, bars 15 and 53.** The one-beat G7 was broken into G and D only, never its B and F; the beat-and-a-half E7 into E, B and D, never its G♯. *Why:* the song's broken chords climb one string an eighth, and a short chord ends before the climb reaches the 3rd; the build was made on 8 November, before the root-and-3rd rule. *Rule:* as There Will Never Be Another You: a chord shorter than two beats plays the bass, then the rest of it together. *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).

- **Long Ago and Far Away, bars 39, 55 and 60 (the second chorus).** The thumb-on-1-and-3 pattern gave a one-beat chord only half of itself: E°7 on beat 4 lost its E, A♭ minor 7 on beat 3 played only its A♭, D♭9 on beat 4 lost its D♭. *Why:* the song's own two-beat pattern was written on 13 November, the day before the Where or When rule, and nobody went back to it. *Rule:* as Where or When: a chord shorter than two beats is struck whole. When a rule is made, look for every build with its own copy of the pattern it fixes. *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).
- **Long Ago and Far Away, bar 4.** The note called B♭7♭9's flat 9th “B”. *Rule:* notes are spelled for the key and the chord: the ♭9 of B♭ is C♭.

- **You'd Be So Nice to Come Home To, the C7 in bar 77 and its grips.** The page called it “the A-shape at the 3rd fret” four times, a CAGED name the owner doesn't use. *Rule:* name a position plainly, by its fret and the string with the root (“barre at the 3rd fret, root on the A string”). *Check:* the night's site check now looks for CAGED shape names on every page (`site_files.py`).
- **The review's sound check, It Keeps You Runnin'.** Every E13 bar was reported as playing a B that isn't in the diagram. The build plays the diagram exactly; the B was the open low E's own overtone (an octave and a fifth up), which this guitar sound makes louder than the E itself. *Rule:* a note heard but not in the diagram counts only away from the diagram's own overtones. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound` (the false B is gone; Barquito's and You'd Be So Nice's real ones still show).

- **Misty, C♯ minor 9 (Intro 2 and 4, bars 6, 8, 14, 24, 30, 31).** The picking went up the D, G and B strings and never reached the open E, the 3rd. *Why:* the kit's flowing pattern takes the first three strings above the bass, whatever notes they hold; the 3rd was on the fourth. *Rule:* a pattern may leave notes out, never the root or the 3rd (14 Nov); when the grip's 3rd sits above the strings a pattern reaches, it leaves out the 5th instead. *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).
- **Misty, bars 15 and 16.** In the one-chord-a-beat turnaround each chord got its bass and its top, so G♯ minor 7, F♯7♭9, C♯ minor 7 and D♯ minor 7 never sounded their 3rds. *Rule:* a chord shorter than two beats is struck whole (Where or When, 14 Nov). *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).
