# Lessons

Each mistake, why it happened, and the rule (and check) that prevents it. Newest first.

## 16 November 2026

- **Misty, C♯ minor 9 (Intro 2 and 4, bars 6, 8, 14, 24, 30, 31).** The picking went up the D, G and B strings and never reached the open E, the 3rd. *Why:* the kit's flowing pattern takes the first three strings above the bass, whatever notes they hold; the 3rd was on the fourth. *Rule:* a pattern may leave notes out, never the root or the 3rd (14 Nov); when the grip's 3rd sits above the strings a pattern reaches, it leaves out the 5th instead. *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).
- **Misty, bars 15 and 16.** In the one-chord-a-beat turnaround each chord got its bass and its top, so G♯ minor 7, F♯7♭9, C♯ minor 7 and D♯ minor 7 never sounded their 3rds. *Rule:* a chord shorter than two beats is struck whole (Where or When, 14 Nov). *Check:* `review.py SLUG` (“plays”).
- **Misty, the singer's track.** Its guitar was another take from the guitar-alone track (the singer's track came from the conversation's page, the guitar from the build), so what he heard with Crosby was not quite what the diagrams show, and the review couldn't take the guitar out to hear Crosby. *Rule:* the guitar-alone and the singer's tracks come from one render. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --voice` now says so when the singer's track holds almost none of the guitar-alone track.
- **The review, Я вас любил (nothing changed).** Hvorostovsky's vibrato swings a semitone, so held notes between B and C read as either, and two pitch readers disagree by half a semitone. *Rule:* a rub on a wavering note is checked by ear before anything changes. *Check:* `review.py --voice` marks such rubs “check by ear”.
- **The review.** It judged each grip of a chord alone, so Misty's B major 9 (bass first, then the top grip with the D♯) and a last chord broken upward by its own run read as missing their 3rds. *Check:* `review.py` now counts every grip of the same chord in a bar, and a run on the chord's own frets.

## 15 November 2026

- **Fly Me to the Moon, bars 10, 26 and 58.** Where Rod holds C over G, the guitar played G7 (or G9) and its B sat under the C for half a second to a second. The record's band holds G9sus4 there and lets it fall only as he falls to B. *Why:* the band's chords were read half a bar at a time, so a suspension that resolves on beat 3 or 4 was read as G7 for the whole bar. *Rule:* where the singer holds a note a half step above a chord tone, read the band beat by beat and hold the suspension until the singer resolves. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --voice` (“rub”); the song's own rub check.
- **Fly Me to the Moon, bar 11.** The note said “E minor 7 at the 7th fret”; the grip is the open one, 020000. *Why:* the note was written for an earlier grip and not read again. *Rule:* every note that names a fret is read against the grip it describes. *Check:* `review.py` (“note”).
- **Sería fantástico, bars 13 and 47.** G♯7♭9 was picked G♯, F♯, A, D♯: the B♯, its 3rd, never sounded. **Bars 29 and 63:** B minor add 11 got its open D, the 3rd, only on the second beat. *Why:* the hand-first picking follows a line through the grip and skipped the string with the 3rd; the diagram showed the note, so nobody missed it by eye. *Rule (agreed 14 Nov):* a pattern may leave notes out, never the root or the 3rd. *Check:* `review.py` (“plays”) reads every pattern on the page and names a chord whose root or 3rd is never struck; `--sound` now looks for the root and the 3rd by their own pitch, not their pitch class, which another note's overtones can fake.
- **Sería fantástico, bar 29.** The note says the open D, B and E ring the whole bar, but the track stopped them at every change of bass, so B minor 11 over A lost its B. *Why:* the renderer ends every note when its chord ends. *Rule:* where a page says strings ring, the track lets them ring across the change (the same string and fret go on). *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound` (root or 3rd not heard).
- **Barquito de papel, the main guitar track (not fixed: needs his yes).** In the two-beat E9 and E7♭9 (bars 15, 17, 23, 25, 45, 47, 55, 57, 63, 65) the G♯, the 3rd, is never played; D6/A loses its D in bars 13, 23, 49 and 53; A6/9 loses its C♯ in bar 48. *Why:* the track was made on 2 November by an earlier build that isn't kept, before the root-and-3rd rule. *Rule:* as above. *Check:* `review.py` now reads Barquito's older page; `--sound` lists the bars.
- **Key Finder, Barquito de papel, bar 39 (not fixed: needs his yes).** The beginner chart folds F♯7 and F♯7♭13 into F♯7, so the piano holds C♯ under Serrat's D for half a second; the guitar's F♯7♭13 has the D. *Why:* the beginner chords have no ♭13, and every F♯7 shape has C♯ near the voice. *Rule (14 Nov):* a simplified chord whose new note rubs against a held sung note stays as it was. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --voice --kf`.
- **Fly Me to the Moon, while fixing it.** The singer's track was locked twice: the import tool locks the file it's given, and it was given the locked copy, so the key opened only another locked file. Caught before it went up. *Rule:* `--locked` takes the plain mp3. *Check:* `tools/shelf_voice.py` refuses a file that is already locked.
- **The site check.** `site_files.py` read the song list with the wrong field names and reported every song missing from it. *Rule:* the check reads `id` and `page`, the fields `song_index.py` writes. *Check:* fixed in `site_files.py`.

## 14 November 2026

- **I Wonder Who's Kissing Her Now, bars 26–27 (a wrong proposal, caught before it went in).** The review read Dean's held note as A against the B♭ of C minor 7 and proposed C minor 6. He sings B♭, a quarter tone flat, and B♭ is in the chord. *Why:* the review tool took the singer's tuning as the median of rounded fractions, which flips a note sung a quarter tone flat to the note below. *Rule:* the singer's tuning is a circular mean over steady frames, and any note that decides a change is measured again with it before the change. *Check:* `review.py` now does this.
- **Marble Halls, bars 34, 38, 78, 82.** The diagram showed E♭ over B♭, but the picking played only the B♭s and G, so the E♭ was never heard and the chord sounded like G minor over B♭. *Why:* the pattern was chosen to keep the E♭ above middle C away from the sung D, and dropped every E♭ with it. *Rule:* a pattern may leave notes of the grip out, but never the root or the 3rd; when one octave is in the way, use the other. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound` (“diagram's … not heard”).
- **How Deep Is the Ocean, compendium, “Replaced” for bars 13 and 29.** Their ▶ played nothing: the notes were written with string numbers (6 for the low E) where the page counts strings from 0. *Rule:* compendium sounds count strings from the low E, 0 to 5. *Check:* `review.py` (“cp sound”).
- **I Wonder Who's Kissing Her Now, Key Finder, bars 24 (both choruses).** The beginner rearrangement turned the song's F minor 6 into F minor 7, so the piano held E♭ a half step above Dean's D on "won-der if" for about four seconds. The guitar page already said an E♭ would rub there. *Why:* the beginner tool folds chords into one kind per root without asking what is sung over the new chord. *Rule:* when a chart is simplified, every chord that changes kind is checked against the held sung notes; a chord whose new tone sits a half step from one is kept as it was. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --voice --kf`.
- **I Wish You Love and What a Diff'rence a Day Made, Fret Finder.** The trumpet lines' "hear it" buttons pointed at the singer's track by its open name after that track went up locked, so they played nothing. *Why:* locking renamed the track in the track list but not in the trumpet part's pointer. *Rule:* when a track is locked, every name that points at it goes too; the trumpet buttons use the open full track. *Check:* `site_files.py` lists every file a page names that isn't in its folder.
- **How Deep Is the Ocean, bar 13.** A7♯9 put a C against Perry's C♯. *Why:* the chord was chosen for a note he doesn't sing, read from an earlier guess instead of his track. *Rule:* read the melody from the singer's own track, never from a MIDI file or a guess. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --voice`.
- **How Deep Is the Ocean, Intro 3.** F7♭9 at the 1st fret when the same chord sat next door at the 8th. *Rule:* choose each grip with the chords around it; jump only for a reason, such as a lower bass. *Check:* `review.py` jumps.

## 14 November 2026

- **I Wish You Love on Rod's record, while it was being built.** The last A walked its bass down G♭, F, E, E♭ on the A string, right in the range where Rod sings F and E♭, so the bass sat a half step above his held notes. *Rule:* keep the bass under the voice; when a bass line walks in the singer's range, move the shapes down the neck (here the low E string, with the open E). *Check:* the rub check in the song's `build_audio.py`, and `review.py SLUG --voice`.
- **I Wish You Love, the same build.** Two-beat chords in the flowing pattern played only two of their three inner notes, so B♭7♭9 lost its A♭ and the line inside E♭ minor (D, D♭, C) was never heard. *Rule:* a short chord plays every inner note once, the notes that moved from the chord before first. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound` (“diagram's … not heard”).
- **I Wish You Love, the same build.** The free-time verse timed chord changes inside a bar from the record, but the page lights a bar's beats evenly, so the dots ran early or late. *Rule:* in free time, write each chord's length in beats so even beats land where the record changes. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound`.
- **Where or When on the new recording, while it was being built.** Where the singer holds C♯ over D, the guitar's lowest D (the open D string, or the A string at the 5th fret) sits a half step above the baritone's C♯, the same octave. *Rule:* when the voice holds a note a half step under the guitar's lowest possible root, take the root out: the iii chord (F♯ minor 7 for D major 9) keeps the colour. *Check:* the song's rub check, `review.py SLUG --voice`.
- **Where or When, the same build.** In two-beat, a chord of one beat that starts on an even beat played only its bass, and one that starts on an odd beat only its top, so C9sus4 and A7sus4 were never heard whole. *Rule:* a chord shorter than two beats in a two-beat pattern is struck whole. *Check:* `review.py SLUG --sound`.
- **Have Your Way, the trumpet part.** It was first moved into the singer's rests; the owner wanted the record's trumpet line where the record plays it. *Rule:* for a record with its own trumpet, the trumpet part follows the record, under the voice where the record does; the rests rule is for trumpet lines the arrangement adds.
