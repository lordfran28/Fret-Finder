Key D majorTime 6/8About 100 eighths a minuteStandard tuning, no capo

Mozart wrote Don Giovanni’s serenade for a mandolin and plucked strings. Here the guitar plays the mandolin part note for note, in D major like your recording. There are no chords to strum, just one line of sixteenths running under your voice. Most of it sits in one place: the D major scale at the 7th fret.

Learn 24 bars, not 44. In verse 2 (bars 25–43) the mandolin plays bars 5–23 again, note for note, then ends on the top D. Bars 1–4 are the intro.

Two hand positions. The 7th fret covers bars 1–9, 17–19 and 21–24. The 2nd fret covers bars 10–16, where each half bar is a chord you hold and play one string at a time. Bar 20 sits at the 3rd fret. Every move between positions happens on an open string or after an eighth note.

Right hand. Alternate index and middle (or use a pick, the way the mandolin does). Play nearer the bridge than usual for a brighter, mandolin-like twang. Let notes ring only within the beat; each bar has two beats of three eighths.

Octave. A guitar sounds an octave below the notes it reads, so this is the mandolin part one octave down. That's the usual way to play it on guitar, and it keeps the line mostly just above your voice (D3 to E4, as written for Don Giovanni).

Hand positions

One finger per fret at the 7th, and a handful of held chords at the 2nd. Every note in the song is on one of these.

Home · the 7th fret

Index on 7, middle on 8, ring on 9, pinky on 10. The index reaches back to the 6th for the C♯ on the G string. The open D string is the low D at the end of bars 4, 8, 22 and 24. Bars 1, 5, 9, 19 and 23 start with the same five notes up the scale: D (G string 7), E (G 9), F♯ (B 7), G (B 8), A (B 10).

The middle · held chords, half a bar each

Hold the shape, then let the line climb and fall through it. The dots are exactly the notes Mozart uses in that half bar.

The words

Lorenzo da Ponte’s two verses, with a plain English line for each.

Practice order

Four sittings. Use the 75% and 50% buttons as much as you like; the tab follows the track at any speed.

  1. The 7th fret: bars 1–9.

    The intro and the first verse line. Play the D scale up and down at the 7th (D E F♯ G A B C♯ D) until the fingers go one per fret on their own, then play bars 1–4 with the track. Bars 5–9 repeat 1–4 with small changes.

  2. The held chords: bars 10–16.

    Hold each shape from the chart before you play it: E, A, D, E, A, F♯ minor, B7, then open strings and a barre at 5. Play each half bar slowly as a broken chord. The line only ever moves through what your hand is already holding.

  3. The climb and the joins: bars 17–24.

    Up to the 7th on the open D (bar 17), the scale to the top D (bars 18–19), down to the 3rd fret (bar 20), back up on the open G (bar 21), and down the D chord to the open D (bar 24). Practise each move on its own: last note before, first note after.

  4. Sing over it.

    Start at 75% with the guide voice. Then sing over “Guitar only”, then play it yourself. Verse 2 is the same guitar part, so once verse 1 holds together, the whole song does.
