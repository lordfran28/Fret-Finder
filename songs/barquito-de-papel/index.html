Right hand

How to play it

Rolling eighths

Most bars: eight even notes. The bass, then the top three strings going up, then the lowest of the upper notes, then back down through the middle. With the bass on the 5th string:

5 · 3 · 2 · 1 · 4 · 2 · 3 · 2

Two chords in a bar: the first four notes for each. With fewer strings in a grip, use the strings you have in the same order.

Under the trumpet

Bars 49–57: lighter, so the trumpet carries the tune. Bass on beat 1, the top three strings together on beats 2 and 4, the next string down on beat 3.

bass · top 3 · 4th · top 3

Held bars

Bars 41–42 and 74–76: roll the chord once, bass first, and let it ring. Halfway through, touch the top two notes again, softly.

The ending

Bars 77–80, in free time: one string at a time, low to high, about an eighth apart, then let it ring. The last chord rolls slowly.

Arrangement notes

What differs from the record

The chords follow the record. These are the few changes, each for a reason.

  • A6/9 at the ends of lines (bars 14, 24, 48, 64). You land on A there, and A maj9’s G♯ would rub against it.
  • Plain F♯m and Bm in the chorus. The tune leans on A and G♯ there, so the grips leave those notes to you.
  • C♯7 → C♯7♭9 in bar 35: one note goes up a fret and comes back down in F♯ minor.
  • A7/G and F♯7♭13 in bars 38–39: the bass walks A, G, F♯, and the ♭13 is the D you sing on beat 4.
  • E6 → E7 → E7/D high on the neck in bars 41–42, climbing with your top notes. The record stays low here.
  • C♯7sus4 in bar 75: Serrat sings F♯ early there, and the sus4 holds it with him.
  • The trumpet lines are written for this arrangement in the places where the flute plays on the record. They aren’t the flute part.

Practice

Practice order

  1. The verse: A maj9 and D6/A over the open A string, then A6/9. Play bars 9–14 with the rolling eighths until the right hand runs by itself.
  2. The verse endings: Bm11 → E9, B7 → E7♭9, and the walk A, G♯, F♯ into the chorus (bar 26).
  3. The chorus: F♯m at the 2nd fret, Bm and E9 at the 7th, C♯7 at the 3rd. Then G♯7sus4 → G♯7 and the C♯7 → C♯7♭9 sigh.
  4. The climb, bars 36–43, slowly, until the jump to the 9th fret for E6 is sure.
  5. The intro (D6/9/F♯ and A maj9) and the falling line at the end.
  6. The whole song with Serrat’s voice, then with the guitar and trumpet track, singing yourself.
