The chords

The main grip for each chord comes first and is outlined. Under it are other ways to play the same chord: shells, three-string shapes, colors, stretches. Tap any grip to hear it.

Getting to the next chord

Several ways for each chord change in the song. Diagrams marked RUN are single notes or pairs played one after another; numbered dots show the order. Every other diagram is a chord you strike.

Play-through

Under the spoken part

While Elvis speaks, the band plays the chorus once more. Play it quietly: bass on beat 1, the three-string shape on beats 2 and 3. Every grip sits between the 1st and 7th fret, and the top notes barely move from bar to bar, like a piano’s right hand.

The song chord by chord. For each chord, every move the piano makes is turned into a guitar grip, in order: the moving chords, the passing chords, the octaves, the inner lines. Each grip keeps the piano's top note on top and as many of its other notes as fit under the hand. The name over each grip is what the piano is actually playing at that moment, so you'll see the B♭7♭9 and B♭13 colors inside a bar that Chordify just calls B♭7. Under that are five ways to play the main chord, then the piano roll if you want to check a spot.

Speed

Grips are worked out from your MIDI file, so where the transcription missed a note, the grip will miss it too. The last part of the recording has the trumpet in it, so moves near the end may be the trumpet's. Grips that sit high on the piano are played an octave lower, on the guitar's top strings.
