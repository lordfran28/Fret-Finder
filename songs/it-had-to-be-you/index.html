The road map

How it goes

  1. 1IntroThree bars, then the pickup bar: you start singing on its 2nd beat. Trumpet plays a quiet line.
  2. 2First chorusYou sing all 32 bars. The trumpet answers you once, in bar 15.
  3. 3Trumpet soloFrom the pickup in bar 32 through bar 15 of the second chorus. You keep playing the same chords, lighter.
  4. 4You come backOn beat 2 of bar 16, with the pickup into the second half, and sing to bar 31.
  5. 5EndingThree bars after your last note: G♭ minor, then D♭. The trumpet closes it.
Before you start

How to play it

One slow roll per chord

Bass first, then the upper notes one at a time, and let them ring. In a one-chord bar, roll on beat 1 and touch the top two notes again on beat 3. In a two-chord bar, one roll each.

Leave the long notes alone

Where you hold a word for a whole bar (bars 1, 3, 7, 9, 11, 15 and the same places later), play the roll and nothing more. The space is the point.

Where it sits on the neck

Bars 1–24 stay between the 1st and 8th frets. At bar 25 the guitar climbs to the 9th fret with the tune, then walks back down one fret at a time to D♭ (move 4).

Under the trumpet

During the solo, roll each chord once and softly, with the first chorus’s grips; only bar 51 thins to the bass and top note, under the trumpet’s low F. Back to full rolls when you sing again.

Practice order

Learn it in this order

  1. The main grips for bars 1–16, one roll per chord, singing along. Moves 1–3 live here.
  2. Bars 17–24: the same chords as 1–8, so this is mostly review.
  3. Bars 25–31 with move 4, slowly, until the walk down needs no looking.
  4. The intro and the ending (move 5), then the whole thing with the trumpet.
Trumpet

The solo spot and the backing lines

What changed from your lead sheet

  • Everything is a half step higher, in D♭, Haymes’s key. The record plays about a third of a semitone sharp, so tune up a little to play along with it.
  • The passing E minor 7 (here F minor 7) in bars 3 and 19 is left out: the note you hold there clashes with it.
  • Bars 11–12 use one chord, F7♯5, where the lead sheet has two altered F chords; its ♯5 is the D♭ you sing there.
  • The intro is the lead sheet’s two-bar intro played twice. The ending (G♭ minor to D♭) is added.
